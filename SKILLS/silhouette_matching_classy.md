# Silhouette matching: Classy style

A classy outfit works because of structure and restraint: a defined waist, clean lines
from shoulder to hem, a tight palette, and a few quality textures. Color and
accessories come after the silhouette. Never pick items by name pairs ("tweed goes
with X"). Tag each item, choose a formula, apply the rules, then check for failure
modes.

## Scope

This skill describes **classy / polished style**: tailored and old-money-leaning
dressing for office, dinner, meetings and smart-casual days. Typical pieces are
tweed and tailored jackets, crisp or satin blouses, high-waist wide or flare
trousers, pencil and satin skirts, long coats, pointed or slingback shoes,
structured bags and gold hardware. The formulas come from that style.

- If the user wants this style (or words like elegant, office, chic, polished,
  old money, business), use this skill as written.
- If the user wants a different style (Asian street fashion, sporty, boho,
  Western streetwear), don't add this style's signature pieces (pussy-bow blouse,
  tweed with gold buttons, structured top-handle bag). The proportion principles
  (waist point, hem and shoe, one dominant volume) still apply.

## Step 1: Tag every item

Tag from product data or the image. If an attribute is unclear, state your
assumption in your reasoning; never invent it silently.

| Attribute | Values |
|---|---|
| Structure | soft / semi-structured / sharply tailored |
| Fit | fitted (nipped waist) / straight / boxy / oversized |
| Jacket or top length | cropped (at waist) / high-hip / hip / longline (mid-thigh) / coat (knee or longer) |
| Rise (bottoms) | mid / high / very high |
| Leg shape | straight / wide / flare / pencil skirt / A-line skirt / bias or fluid skirt |
| Length | ankle / full (hem to the shoe) / floor (breaks over the heel) / mini / knee / midi / maxi |
| Fabric | tweed or bouclé, wool or suiting, satin or silk, fine knit, crisp cotton, denim, leather or suede |
| Neck and cuff details | collar, pussy-bow, scarf, contrast cuff, rolled cuff, stand collar |
| Sleeve | fitted / rolled / puffed or balloon (gathered cuff) / sheer |
| Shoe | pointed pump / slingback / Mary Jane block heel / ankle boot; heel: low / mid / high |
| Bag | structured top-handle / shoulder baguette / clutch / bucket; size |
| Hardware and metals | gold / silver / none (buttons, bag lock, jewelry) |
| Color | one of: neutral (black, cream, beige, grey, navy, brown), accent (burgundy, light blue, etc.), pattern (polka dot, stripe, houndstooth) |

## Step 2: Choose one formula and name it

**A. Structured top, wide leg**
Fitted or nipped jacket/blouse ending at the waist or high hip, with high-rise
wide or flare trousers (or wide jeans for a casual version).
Requires: high rise; a visible waist point (nipped jacket, belt, tuck); hem long
enough to nearly touch the floor in heels.
Why: the fitted top and the long fluid leg make a tall inverted shape; the
tailoring keeps it polished.

**B. Cropped jacket, high waist**
Boxy or semi-fitted cropped jacket (tweed, bouclé) with a shirt or blouse
underneath, tucked into high-waist trousers or jeans, with a belt.
Requires: jacket ends at the waist or just below; blouse tucked and belt visible
or a clear waistband; collar or cuff showing at the edges.
Why: the short jacket exposes the waistband, so the waist point is clear; the
shirt collar adds a clean line at the neck.

**C. Tonal column**
Same color family from top to bottom (cream on cream, black on black, beige on
beige), with texture contrast inside the column: tweed over satin, wool over
suiting.
Requires: at least two different textures; one detail that breaks the line (gold
buttons, belt, pale contrast collar or cuff).
Why: an unbroken tone lengthens the body, and the texture keeps it from looking flat.

**D. Hourglass and pencil**
Fitted jacket or top ending at the natural waist or just below, with a high-waist
pencil or straight midi skirt.
Requires: jacket hem meets the skirt waistband without a gap; skirt hem at mid-calf;
pointed heel.
Why: the waist is the narrowest point, and the skirt line is clean and vertical.

**E. Fluid skirt, structured top**
A satin or bias skirt (midi or maxi) with a tailored blazer or a statement blouse
(balloon sleeves, bow, peplum).
Requires: one volume only: either the sleeve or the skirt carries movement, not
both at full strength; waist defined (peplum, tuck, belt or blazer button).
Why: the drape of satin against structure or sleeve volume gives contrast.

**F. Long layer over column**
Long coat, longline blazer or open duster over a slim or straight base (turtleneck
or cardigan with high-waist trousers) and a belt under the coat.
Requires: the coat is the one big volume; the base underneath stays slim; shoe is
slim (ankle boot or pointed pump).
Why: the vertical line of the coat lengthens the figure, and the belt under it
keeps a waist point.

## Step 3: Apply the core rules

1. **Waist is always defined.** A nipped jacket, belt, tuck, peplum or the waistband
   of a skirt. A straight, unmarked line from shoulder to hip reads as unfinished.
2. **High rise** with wide, flare and pencil bottoms.
3. **Jacket length is a choice, not a default.** Cropped (waist) or longline
   (mid-thigh, nipped). A jacket that ends exactly at the widest part of the hip, with
   no waist shaping, cuts the body in half.
4. **Hem and shoe.** Wide and flare trousers fall to the floor in heels, covering
   most of the shoe with only the pointed toe or the heel showing. Ankle trousers need a
   deliberate gap and a visible shoe.
5. **Shoe is slim and pointed (or Mary Jane block heel), even with wide legs.**
   Elegance comes from heel height and a refined toe. Chunky sneakers or heavy
   platforms break the style.
6. **Layers show at the edges.** Collar at the neck, a different-colored cuff at
   the wrist (cream cuff on a tweed jacket, white cuff rolled back), shirt hem
   under a knit. This adds lines without bulk.
7. **Palette: three colors at most.** One or two neutrals and one accent, or a tonal
   set. The bag repeats the shoe or one main color. An accent (burgundy, pale blue)
   goes in one place, or in two places that echo (burgundy top and burgundy shoes).
8. **Texture contrast.** Pair tweed, wool or suiting with satin, silk or fine
   cotton. Structure plus fluidity is the core of this style.
9. **Hardware matches.** All metals in the outfit are the same tone (gold buttons,
   gold bag lock, gold earrings). Don't mix gold and silver.
10. **Accessories: one to three, and they're quality pieces.** A structured top-handle
    or shoulder bag, a watch or fine bracelet, earrings. A scarf or bow at the neck is
    a feature, so keep jewelry smaller then.
11. **Pattern: one.** Polka dot, stripe or houndstooth is the single pattern in
    the outfit; everything else is solid.

---

## Step 4: Check failure modes (reject or fix)

- Cropped boxy jacket with a low-rise bottom → the waist line is lost. Fix: high rise.
- Hip-length unshaped jacket + wide trousers + no belt → shapeless.
- Wide or flare trousers too short for the heel → awkward hem. Fix: longer length
  or a different shoe.
- Chunky sneakers, platforms or flat bare sandals in a tailored look → style break.
  (Exception: if the user asks for casual, a clean low-profile sneaker or loafer
  can replace the heel, but tell them it lowers the formality.)
- Gold and silver hardware together.
- Two patterns, or a pattern plus heavy texture on every piece → busy.
- Satin top + satin skirt in the same tone → too shiny, no contrast.
- Big sleeve volume + wide fluid skirt + big bag → two or three competing volumes.
- All black with no texture or tone variety → flat. Fix: tweed, satin, suede or a
  pale collar.
- Long coat over wide trousers with no waist marked and no slim layer under → bulky.
- Too many accessories (scarf + big necklace + statement earrings + brooch).

---

## Step 5: Adjust to the person and the occasion

Treat these as goals, not restrictions. Follow the user's stated preferences first.

- **Petite:** prefer cropped jackets (A/B), very high rise, trousers with a heel
  to the floor, tonal column (C). Avoid long coats unless they're fitted; avoid maxi
  skirts without heels.
- **Tall:** can carry longline blazers, maxi skirts, coats (F), and flare trousers.
- **Wants to show the waist:** prefer D, A, or B with a belt.
- **Prefers a relaxed middle:** prefer C or F with a straight cut and a belt under
  a long open layer.
- **Office / formal:** keep the formula; use suiting, tweed, satin blouse, slingback
  or pointed pump, structured bag.
- **Smart-casual:** wide-leg jeans in a light or mid wash with a tailored blazer (A or B), a crisp
  white shirt and a pointed pump or loafer.
- **Evening:** satin skirt (E), sheer or ribbon blouse, stiletto, small clutch.
- **Cold weather:** coat over a column (F); or tweed jacket with wool trousers
  and an ankle boot with a slim heel. The coat or jacket must follow the same
  rules.
- **Warm weather:** drop the outer layer; a satin or silk blouse with trousers or
  a midi skirt, slingbacks, one metal tone.

---

## Output format

For each outfit:
1. Items, with key tags (e.g. "cream tweed jacket, fitted, cropped at waist, gold buttons").
2. Formula name (A–F).
3. One sentence on why it works (the waist point, the hem and shoe, the palette).
4. Optional: one swap if the user wants it more casual or more formal.

## Worked examples

**1 · Formula B**
Navy cropped tweed jacket, collarless, boxy; light blue collared shirt tucked in,
thin black belt. High-rise mid-blue wide-flare jeans. Black patent Mary Jane block
heels. Navy bucket bag.
Why: the jacket ends at the belt, so the waist is exposed; the pale blue collar
shows at the neck; the flare covers most of the heel.

**2 · Formula C**
Cream fitted tweed jacket with gold buttons, light blue collar peeking out. Cream
high-waist straight trousers. Cream pointed slingbacks. Pale blue quilted clutch.
Why: a single tone from top to hem lengthens the figure; the gold buttons and the
blue collar add detail; the clutch repeats the collar color.

**3 · Formula A**
Burgundy collared knit cardigan, tucked, with a black belt. Black very wide-leg
high-waist trousers. Burgundy pointed slingbacks. Black structured bag.
Why: the accent sits at the top and at the shoe, so the eye moves down the
outfit; the belt marks the waist under the knit; the trouser covers the heel.

**4 · Formula B (relaxed variant)**
Brown oversized shirt, sleeves rolled, small scarf at the neck, tucked and belted.
Cream wide high-waist trousers. Cream slingbacks. Dark brown structured bag.
Why: the tuck and belt give the oversized shirt a waist; the scarf adds a vertical
accent; brown and cream is a two-tone palette; the bag repeats the shirt.

**5 · Formula E**
Black sheer balloon-sleeve blouse with a peplum and ribbon ties. White satin bias
maxi skirt. Black stiletto pumps. Black small ruffled clutch.
Why: the sleeve is the volume at the top and the satin skirt is fluid, not
full; the peplum seam marks the waist; the heel carries the long hem.

**6 · Formula A (with scarf)**
Black tailored blazer with a gold button and a striped scarf tied at the neck.
White satin skirt with black polka dots, midi. Black slingbacks. Black shoulder bag.
Why: the polka dot is the only pattern, and the black blazer ends at the hip with
a nipped waist; the scarf is the feature at the neck, so no necklace.

**7 · Formula F**
Long black wool coat, open. Black turtleneck, belt. Black high-waist straight
trousers. Black ankle boots with a slim heel. Black shoulder bag.
Why: the coat is the single volume and its vertical line lengthens; the slim base
and belt keep the waist visible; the boot is slim, not heavy. Add a texture
change (suede boot or wool coat against a fine knit) so all-black isn't flat.

**8 · Formula A (open jacket)**
Cream satin pussy-bow blouse with puffed sleeves and a gathered cuff. Black tweed cropped
jacket worn open over the shoulders. Black high-waist wide-flare trousers. Black
patent Mary Jane block heels. Black top-handle bag, gold earrings and watch.
Why: the satin against the tweed is a texture contrast; the blouse is tucked at the
waist; the bow is the neck detail; all metals are gold.

**9 · Formula A (casual)**
Beige houndstooth longline blazer, nipped at the waist, sleeves rolled to show white
cuffs. White crisp shirt. Light-wash wide-leg jeans. Nude suede pointed pumps.
Cream structured top-handle bag, gold earrings.
Why: the nipped waist makes a shape in a hip-length jacket; the white cuff and the
shirt collar are the edge layers; tonal nude shoe and cream bag keep the palette
quiet.

**10 · Formula D**
Grey fitted jacket, asymmetric collar, cropped at the waist with a V hem. Grey
high-waist pencil midi skirt with a button front. Snakeskin pointed pumps. Tan
structured bag. Long thin pendant necklace.
Why: the jacket meets the skirt waistband, so the waist is the narrowest point; the
tan bag and the snake print pick up the warm neutral; the long pendant adds a
vertical line.

**11 · Formula F (smart-casual)**
Long black coat, open. Grey cardigan over a white V-neck shirt, black belt. Cream
pleated wide trousers. Cream clutch.
Why: the belt marks the waist under the coat; the grey and white layers show at the
edges; the cream trousers lighten the long dark coat.
