# Silhouette matching: Minimalism

A minimalist outfit works through restraint: few colors, no logos or prints, clean lines,
and one deliberate contrast, either of fit (fitted against oversized) or of
texture (knit against leather). Every piece is either clearly fitted or clearly
oversized, never in between. Never pick items by name pairs ("turtleneck goes with
X"). Tag each item, choose a formula, apply the rules, then check for failure
modes.

## Scope

This skill describes **minimalist style**, mainly 90s minimalism and its modern
capsule version: fine-knit turtlenecks and tanks, white shirts and tees, straight
jeans, pencil and column skirts, straight or wide trousers, oversized blazers,
trench and belted wool coats, leather jackets, thin belts, kitten heels, slingbacks,
slim ankle boots, and one simple bag.

- If the user wants this style (or words like minimal, clean, capsule, basic,
  quiet, 90s, understated), use this skill as written.
- If the user wants a different style (classy/tailored with tweed and gold hardware,
  Asian street fashion, boho, sporty), keep the proportion principles (fitted
  against oversized, hem and shoe, one texture focal) but don't strip the
  signature pieces of that style.

## Step 1: Tag every item

Tag from product data or the image. If an attribute is unclear, state your
assumption in your reasoning; never invent it silently.

| Attribute | Values |
|---|---|
| Fit | fitted / straight / relaxed / oversized |
| Top type | turtleneck / mock neck / crew knit / V-neck knit / tank / tee / shirt / wrap top / sheer layer |
| Top length | cropped / waist / hip / longline |
| Sleeve | sleeveless / short / long / rolled |
| Rise (bottoms) | low / mid / high |
| Leg shape | straight / bootcut / flare / wide (pleated) / pencil skirt / column skirt / slip or bias skirt / mini |
| Length | ankle / full (brushes the shoe) / mini / knee / midi (mid-calf) / maxi |
| Fabric | fine knit, chunky knit, cotton jersey, crisp poplin, wool, suiting, satin, leather, croc-embossed leather, denim, sheer mesh |
| Outer layer | belted wool coat / trench / oversized blazer / leather biker / quilted or casual jacket |
| Shoe | kitten-heel slingback / strappy minimal sandal / mule / slim ankle boot / knee boot / flat Mary Jane / clean sneaker / slide |
| Bag | structured tote / baguette shoulder bag / hobo / clutch / straw (casual) |
| Small items | thin belt (black, white, brown) / watch / glasses / sunglasses / simple earrings |
| Color | neutral (black, white, grey, beige, navy, brown, olive) / accent (burgundy, light blue) |

## Step 2: Choose one formula and name it

**A. Fitted top, straight leg**
Fitted knit, tee or tank with straight or bootcut jeans or trousers, mid or low rise
allowed (90s), with a thin belt at the waist.
Requires: the top is truly fitted; leg is straight, not baggy; belt or tuck
defines where the top ends; hem brushes the shoe.
Why: the body line is clean from neck to hem, with nothing extra.

**B. Oversized layer, slim base**
Oversized blazer, leather jacket or relaxed knit over a slim base (tee, tank, fitted
knit) with straight jeans or a straight leg.
Requires: one oversized piece only; the base underneath is fitted or plain; shoulder
line is clean; sleeves can be pushed up or rolled.
Why: one area of volume looks intentional, and the slim base keeps the outfit
sharp.

**C. Column skirt**
Fitted knit, turtleneck or tank with a pencil or column skirt (knee to midi, to
maxi).
Requires: top fitted or only slightly relaxed; skirt hem at knee or mid-calf (or the
floor for maxi); slim shoe or boot; thin belt at the waist if the knit is long.
Why: the vertical line is unbroken and the figure looks longer.

**D. Wide trouser, fitted top**
Pleated wide or flare trousers (high or mid rise) with a fitted long-sleeve, tank,
or a short crew knit.
Requires: top ends at the waist or is tucked; trouser hem covers most of the shoe;
fine fabric (not heavy).
Why: the fitted top and the long wide leg balance each other, and the waist is
visible.

**E. Tonal column with one texture**
One color family (all black, grey and charcoal, beige and cream, brown), with
exactly one texture contrast: leather, croc-embossed leather, sheer layer, wool
against fine knit.
Requires: at least two fabrics in the outfit; a thin belt or a shoe in the same
tone; one visible edge (collar, cuff, tank under a sheer layer).
Why: the unbroken tone lengthens the figure; the texture keeps it from looking flat.

**F. Long coat over slim base**
Belted wool coat, trench or long blazer over a slim base (turtleneck or tee with
straight jeans, trousers or a column skirt).
Requires: the coat is the only volume; the base is slim; the belt is tied or the
coat is open over a defined waist; slim shoe or boot.
Why: the coat's vertical line lengthens the figure, and the base shows the waist.

---

## Step 3: Apply the core rules

1. **Fit is a decision.** Each piece is fitted or oversized. Shapeless "almost
   fitted" or "almost loose" pieces fail. Max one oversized piece per outfit, except
   wide trousers (D) which take a fitted top.
2. **Palette: two or three colors.** Neutrals (black, white, grey, beige, navy,
   brown, olive). One accent allowed (burgundy, light blue) in one piece, or two
   that echo (burgundy knit and burgundy shoes).
3. **No logos, no prints, no graphic text.** Stripes (fine pinstripe) are the
   only pattern and count as texture.
4. **One texture focal.** Leather, croc-embossed leather, sheer, or chunky knit. The
   other pieces are plain.
5. **Define the waist** with a thin belt, a tuck, a wrap top, or the waistband of
   a skirt. The belt matches the shoe or the bag (black belt with black shoes,
   brown with brown, white belt for a white skirt or light shoe).
6. **Hem and shoe.** Straight and bootcut trousers brush the top of the shoe.
   Wide trousers cover most of it. Skirts end at the knee or mid-calf (or the floor for
   maxi); a pencil skirt ending at the widest part of the calf is wrong.
7. **Shoes are slim and simple.** Kitten-heel slingback, minimal strappy sandal, mule,
   slim ankle boot, knee boot. Casual days: a clean sneaker (white leather, low
   profile), flat Mary Jane, slide.
8. **Layers show at the edges.** White tee or tank under a blazer, an open shirt over
   a tank, a collar over a knit. This adds line without bulk.
9. **Necklines are clean.** Turtleneck, mock neck, crew, V-neck, sleeveless
   turtleneck. Sheer is allowed only over a tank or one clean layer.
10. **Accessories: one bag and up to two small items.** A structured tote or a
    baguette bag in black or brown; a watch, glasses or sunglasses, small earrings.
    No visible logos, no heavy hardware.
11. **Metals: one tone** (silver watch with silver belt buckle, or gold with gold).

## Step 4: Check failure modes (reject or fix)

- Oversized top + wide or baggy bottom with no waist point → shapeless.
- Two oversized pieces (oversized blazer + oversized sweater under it).
- A logo, slogan or loud print on any piece.
- More than three colors, or an accent in three places.
- Chunky or heavy shoe with a pencil or column skirt (except boots in cold weather).
- Wide trousers too short for the shoe → awkward hem.
- Pencil skirt hem too tight to walk, or ending at the widest part of the calf.
- All pieces in the same flat fabric, same tone → no depth. Fix: add one texture
  (leather, sheer, wool).
- Mixed metals (silver watch, gold belt buckle, silver glasses).
- Shiny or cheap-looking fabric in a style that depends on fabric quality.
- Hoodie or sweatpants with sneakers and a casual bag with no tailored piece → looks like
  loungewear. Fix: add a coat, blazer or leather jacket.

---

## Step 5: Capsule check (when building a wardrobe)

A minimalist capsule works when every top works with every bottom.

- **Base palette:** black, white, grey, beige, one of navy or brown; one or two accent
  colors in a few pieces (burgundy cardigan, light blue shirt, olive jacket).
- **Tops (8–10):** 2–3 fitted knits (a turtleneck in black and in beige or grey), 1 sheer
  or mock-neck, 2 tanks or tees (white, black or navy), 1–2 white or blue shirts, 1–2
  relaxed sweaters.
- **Bottoms (5–6):** straight dark jeans, a light-wash straight jean, black straight
  or bootcut trousers, grey or black pleated wide trousers, a pencil or column
  skirt, one satin or slip skirt.
- **Outer layers (3–4):** belted wool coat or trench, oversized blazer, leather
  jacket, optionally a quilted or casual jacket.
- **Shoes (4–5):** kitten-heel slingback, slim ankle boot, strappy sandal or mule,
  clean sneaker, flat Mary Jane.
- **Bags and small items:** one tote, one baguette or hobo bag, a thin belt in black and
  brown (or white), a watch, glasses.
- **Test:** each piece has at least three outfits in the capsule; if it has fewer,
  swap it.

---

## Step 6: Adjust to the person and the occasion

Treat these as goals, not restrictions. Follow the user's stated preferences first.

- **Petite:** keep fitted tops and mid or high rise; column skirts at the knee or
  below the knee; a short or cropped blazer; a heel (kitten or block) helps.
- **Tall:** can carry maxi skirts, long coats, wide trousers, and low rise.
- **Wants to show the waist:** prefer A, C or D with a thin belt or a wrap top.
- **Prefers a relaxed middle:** prefer B or F with a long open layer and straight
  jeans.
- **Office:** use the formula; wool trousers or pencil skirt, white shirt or fine
  knit, blazer, slingback or ankle boot, structured tote.
- **Evening:** slip or satin skirt, sheer layer over a tank, strappy sandal, small
  bag.
- **Casual:** straight jeans, tee or relaxed knit, white sneaker or flat, hoodie or
  cardigan only with a tailored layer on top.
- **Cold weather:** coat over a column (F); turtleneck + leather skirt or trousers +
  knee boots (E).
- **Warm weather:** tank or tee, linen or white skirt, mule or strappy sandal, straw
  bag as the one casual bag.

---

## Output format

For each outfit:
1. Items, with key tags (e.g. "black fitted turtleneck, fine knit; dark straight
   jeans, mid rise").
2. Formula name (A–F).
3. One sentence on why it works (the fit contrast, the waist point, the texture).
4. Optional: one swap if the user wants it dressier or more casual.

## Worked examples

**1 · Formula A**
Light blue poplin shirt, tucked. Dark straight cropped jeans, mid rise. Black ankle
socks and flats. Simple watch.
Why: the shirt tucked in shows the waist; the straight leg stays clean; light blue is
the only color.

**2 · Formula E**
Black fitted turtleneck. Black blazer, structured. Brown leather trousers with a belt.
Black bag.
Why: the black base and brown leather make a tonal outfit with one texture focal; the
belt marks the waist; the blazer is the only layer.

**3 · Formula B**
Grey-brown oversized blazer, open, over a white tank and an open cream shirt.
Mid-wash straight jeans. Sunglasses.
Why: the blazer is the one oversized piece; the tank and shirt show at the edge; the
straight jean is slim against it.

**4 · Formula C**
Navy chunky turtleneck. Brown croc-embossed leather pencil skirt, knee length. Knee
boots or pointed pumps.
Why: the skirt is the texture focal; the knit is the only volume; the hem sits at the
knee.

**5 · Formula B (leather)**
Black oversized leather jacket. Black tee. Black belt. Black wide-leg trousers. Black
structured top-handle bag.
Why: the jacket is the volume; the tee and belt define the waist; all black is
broken by leather against jersey and wool.

**6 · Formula C (sheer variant)**
Grey sheer V-neck tank. White knit midi skirt. Minimal strappy sandals.
Why: the sheer top gives the texture; the skirt is column-straight; two colors only.

**7 · Formula C (long)**
Black turtleneck with a thin belt. Navy long straight skirt. Knee boots.
Why: the belt marks the waist over a column line; the boot continues the vertical
line; black and navy are close tones.

**8 · Formula C (sleeveless)**
Black sleeveless turtleneck. Black column maxi skirt, floor length. Slim pointed
shoe.
Why: the sleeveless knit shows the shoulder line; the maxi skirt is the same tone
and falls straight.

**9 · Formula D**
Black fitted long-sleeve top, tucked. Grey pleated wide trousers. Black ankle boots
or a slingback. Black baguette bag.
Why: the fitted top is tucked to show the waist; the trouser covers most of the
shoe; grey and black are one family.

**10 · Formula F**
Beige trench, belted. Grey fitted turtleneck. Dark straight jeans. Black slim ankle
boots. Black tote.
Why: the belted trench is the only volume; the slim base shows the waist; the palette
is beige, grey, black.

**11 · Formula F (long wool)**
Black belted wool coat, long. Black turtleneck. Black pencil midi skirt. Black
kitten-heel slingbacks. Thin silver watch.
Why: the coat and the skirt make a long vertical line; the belt shows the waist; the
slingback is slim.

**12 · Formula B (casual capsule)**
Grey oversized crew sweater. Light-wash straight jeans. White low-profile sneakers.
Black tote.
Why: one relaxed piece on top; the straight jean is the clean base; sneaker and tote
are plain.
