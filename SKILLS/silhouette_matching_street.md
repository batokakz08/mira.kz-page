# Silhouette matching: Street fashion

An outfit works or fails mostly on its silhouette: where the volume sits, where the
lines fall, and how the pieces meet. Color comes after. Never pick items by
name pairs ("flares go with X"). Tag each item, choose a variant and a formula,
apply the rules, then check for failure modes.

## Scope

This skill describes **street fashion** in four variants:

1. **Korean-influenced street:** oversized cropped knits, wide, barrel and flare
   jeans, preppy layering (shirt collars, ties), chunky sneakers and platforms, and one
   saturated accent color against neutrals.
2. **Baggy urban street:** very wide, low-slung jeans; ties and collars under oversized
   sweatshirts, rugby knits and jerseys; flannel or sweaters tied at the hip; cargo
   shorts; tiny bags.
3. **Y2K mini and boots:** mini skirts and shorts with tall slouchy boots, low-rise flare
   jeans, off-shoulder knits, fur, belted trenches and cropped jackets.
4. **Alt / dark street:** graphic tees and long-sleeves, black or washed-black wide
   jeans (embroidered or skull print), studded belts, beanies, skate and retro-runner
   sneakers, one accent color.

Pick one variant before choosing a formula:

| Variant | When | Formulas |
|---|---|---|
| Korean-influenced | cropped knits, preppy layers, one saturated accent | A–F |
| Baggy urban | very wide or low-slung jeans, ties, jerseys, plaid at the hip | G, H |
| Y2K mini and boots | mini skirt or shorts, tall boots, low-rise flare, off-shoulder, fur | I, J |
| Alt / dark | black or charcoal palette, graphic tops, studded belts | G (with the dark rules) |

Mixing variants is fine only when the outfit still follows one formula.

- If the user wants this style, or hasn't named a style and fits its audience,
  use this skill as written.
- If the user wants a different style (classic, minimalist, office, evening), the
  proportion principles still apply (crop line, waist point, hem and shoe, shoe
  weight follows volume), but don't add this style's signature pieces such as ties
  over sweaters, leg warmers, tied flannel or statement-print denim.

## Step 1: Tag every item

Tag from product data or the image. If an attribute is unclear, state your
assumption in your reasoning; never invent it silently.

| Attribute | Values |
|---|---|
| Volume | fitted / relaxed / oversized |
| Top length | cropped (above natural waist) / waist / high-hip / hip / mid-thigh / long |
| Rise (bottoms) | low / mid / high |
| Leg shape | skinny / slim / straight / wide / baggy / barrel (curved, tapered ankle) / flare / bootcut / cargo |
| Leg length | cropped / ankle / full / pooling (breaks over the shoe) / cuffed / cargo shorts / long shorts (below the knee) |
| Denim wash and surface | raw dark / washed grey / light / black / patched / embroidered or skull print / cargo pockets |
| Skirt shape + length | pencil, A-line, pleated, tiered, wrap, puffball; mini / knee / midi / maxi |
| Sleeve | fitted / dropped shoulder / balloon (gathered cuff) / off-shoulder / ruched |
| Texture / weight | fine knit, chunky knit, fuzzy/bouclé, sherpa, crisp cotton, denim, leather, suede, faux fur, puffer, rugby knit, ombré knit, ribbed |
| Shoe weight | delicate / medium / chunky-platform; shaft: low / ankle / mid-calf / knee / over-the-knee |
| Footwear type | chunky sneaker / skate sneaker / retro runner / track sneaker / platform / lace-up boot / slouchy knee boot / over-the-knee boot / cowboy boot / shearling mini boot / heel |
| Waist or hip anchor | belt, studded belt, chain, sash, wrap ties, tied shirt, tied flannel or sweater, drawstring, visible hem of an under-layer, none |
| Graphic | none / small logo / large graphic or text / stripe / plaid |
| Headwear | beanie / cap / beret / headband / bandana / none |
| Hosiery | tights / sheer tights / crew socks / none |
| Outer | puffer or bomber / faux fur or fur-trim / belted trench / cropped bolero / zip track jacket / denim jacket / none |
| Bag scale | mini crossbody / medium shoulder / large hobo or tote |

## Step 2: Choose one formula and name it

**A. Slim up, volume down**
Fitted top (or fitted base + fitted wrap layer) with a wide, barrel or flare leg.
Requires: waist marked (tuck, wrap tie, crop or belt); high rise.
Why: the leg is the statement; a fitted top keeps the upper body clean.

**B. Volume up, slim down**
Oversized or long top with a slim or straight leg, or a short fitted skirt.
Requires: slim bottom shows the leg line; shoe can be lighter.
Why: one area of volume reads as intentional; the slim base grounds it.

**C. Controlled volume on volume**
Oversized top and wide/barrel/full leg together.
Requires ALL of:
1. Top ends at waist or high hip and never covers the rise.
2. A visible waist point (belt, sash, tied shirt, hem of an under-layer).
3. High-rise bottom.
4. Chunky or substantial shoe.
Why: the crop line and waist point give the body a shape inside the volume.
Without them it reads as shapeless.

**D. Fit-and-flare (X shape)**
Cropped top ending at the natural waist with a full skirt (A-line, pleated midi).
Requires: hem of the top meets the skirt's waistband exactly; skirt is high-waisted.
Why: the narrowest point sits at the waist, with volume above and below.

**E. Volume / bare leg / volume** (short skirts)
Oversized top and a short skirt, with volume or weight returning at the leg:
leg warmers, tall boots, platforms.
Why: the bare leg is the slim section; the lower leg needs weight so the outfit
isn't top-heavy.

**F. Column**
Similar tone and similar volume from top to bottom, optional long outer layer.
Why: one unbroken line lengthens the figure. Works fitted or relaxed.

**G. Low-slung baggy (hip anchor)**
Oversized or standard top with very wide, baggy, low-slung or cargo bottoms (jeans,
cargo shorts, drawstring pants).
Requires ALL of:
1. A hip anchor: belt or studded belt, chain, drawstring, or a flannel/sweater tied at
   the hip. If the top is cropped, the waistband and belt are the anchor.
2. A cropped, tucked or short top (ends at or above the anchor), or an oversized top
   with a clear hem line that breaks the length (shirt hem under a sweatshirt).
3. A cuffed or pooling hem that falls over the shoe, wide enough to cover a heavy shoe.
4. A heavy shoe (skate, retro runner, track or chunky sneaker).
Why: in this variant the hip is where the outfit is anchored, and the baggy leg is
the volume; the short top and heavy shoe frame it.

**H. Layered prep-street**
Collared shirt with a tie under an oversized layer (sweatshirt, rugby knit, jersey,
denim jacket, bomber), with a plaid shirt or skirt tied or layered at the hip and
baggy bottoms.
Requires: collar and tie visible between layers; the tie hangs as a vertical
line; one pattern only (plaid or stripe, not both); baggy bottoms plus sneakers.
Why: the tie and collar add vertical lines to a wide outfit; the hip layer gives the
anchor.

**I. Mini and tall boot (Y2K)**
Mini skirt or short shorts with a tall boot (slouchy knee boot, lace-up boot, over-the-knee
boot) or heels with sheer tights.
Requires: weight at the leg (boots with a visible shaft or lace-up); the top is
either fitted/cropped or a drape (off-shoulder, halter) that ends above the skirt hem;
if a long coat or trench is worn, the shorts or skirt stay short under it.
Why: the bare leg is the slim section between the mini hem and the boot top; the
boot brings the weight back.

**J. Low-rise flare (Y2K)**
Fitted tank, tee or cropped knit with low-rise flare or bootcut jeans or trousers,
with a short jacket (bolero, cropped trench, fur-trim or puffer).
Requires: top fitted or cropped so the waistband or hip line shows; flare long
enough to cover most of the shoe; heeled boot or platform; the jacket ends at the
waist or high hip.
Why: the fitted top and short jacket keep the upper body compact, so the flare
from the knee down is the statement.

## Step 3: Apply the core rules

1. **Crop line.** With any wide or full bottom, the top ends at the waist or high
   hip. A top that ends at mid-hip, at the widest point of a wide pant, cuts the
   body in half. (In G the hip anchor replaces this; see rule 11.)
2. **One visible waist point**, unless using formula F. In G and H the hip anchor is
   that point.
3. **High rise** with wide, barrel, flare and full skirts in formulas A–F. Low rise
   is allowed only in G and J (rule 10).
4. **Hem and shoe.** Full-length legs fall over the shoe (flares cover most of
   it; wide legs pool slightly). Ankle-length wide pants need a deliberate gap
   (visible sock or boot).
5. **Shoe weight follows volume.** Bigger volume needs a heavier shoe (chunky
   sneaker, platform, lug boot). Delicate shoes go with slim or fitted outfits.
6. **Layers show at the edges.** Collar at the neck, shirt hem below the
   sweater, tank at an off-shoulder neckline. Adds lines without bulk.
7. **Vertical accents** (tie, sash, long wrap ties, long necklace) break up
   width when there is a lot of volume.
8. **Gathered ends.** Balloon sleeves close at the wrist; barrel legs taper at
   the ankle. A closed end gives volume a shape.
9. **Texture contrast.** Pair a soft or fuzzy volume piece with a structured piece
   (denim, crisp cotton, leather), so the outfit doesn't look like loungewear.
10. **Low rise is allowed only in G and J.** In G the baggy leg needs a hip anchor; in
    J the top must be fitted or cropped. Low rise with a long, loose top and no
    anchor is a failure (see Step 4).
11. **Hip anchor for baggy bottoms.** Belt, chain, tied flannel or sweater, or a
    drawstring that shows. Without it the outfit has no center.
12. **Hem treatment is chosen.** Baggy jeans are either cuffed once (clean line
    above the sneaker) or pooling over the shoe. Cargo shorts and long shorts end
    at or below the knee, with crew socks or a visible ankle gap.
13. **One graphic or logo.** Graphic top with plain bottoms, or graphic or
    embroidered jeans with a plain top. Tie, plaid and stripe each count as a pattern.
14. **One pattern at a time.** Plaid or stripe, not both; a plaid tied at the hip counts
    as the pattern.
15. **Bag scale contrasts or matches the leg.** Mini crossbody with very baggy
    bottoms; medium or large hobo with minis, shorts and boots. A mini bag in the
    accent color (red) works well.
16. **Outer volume needs a slim or tall lower half.** Fur, puffer or oversized coat
    with a mini and boots, flare jeans, or a fitted base. Fur or puffer plus baggy
    jeans plus a large bag is three volumes (see Step 4); keep the bag small.
17. **Off-shoulder and drape tops** pair with a short or fitted bottom (mini, shorts,
    skinny jeans) or a low flare. A strap or tank under it keeps the neckline
    controlled.
18. **Mini plus boot.** Show bare leg or sheer tights between hem and boot top.
    Slouchy boots take mini skirts and shorts; over-the-knee boots take shorts under
    a longer coat.
19. **Sneakers by variant.** Baggy urban: skate shoes or chunky sneakers with crew
    socks. Alt/dark: skate, retro runner or track sneaker in black, brown or white.
    Y2K: boots or heels, sneakers only with baggy bottoms.
20. **Dark palette (alt):** black, charcoal and washed black; one accent (red, burgundy,
    silver hardware). Brown plus black plus gold is an accepted warm variant. Texture
    (denim wash, knit, leather) replaces color for contrast.
21. **Headwear is the finishing detail,** not an extra layer: beanie, cap, beret or
    headband. One only, and it matches the palette.
22. **Jewelry stacks only with plain tops.** Layered necklaces, bangles and rings
    on a solid sweater or tank; none with a large graphic.

## Step 4: Check failure modes (reject or fix)

- Loose top covering the rise + wide leg, no waist point → shapeless. Fix: crop, tuck, belt.
- Hip-length top ending at the widest part of wide pants → body cut in half.
- Wide or flare leg that is too short for the shoe → awkward hem. Fix: longer length or a boot.
- Big volume with a delicate shoe → top-heavy, shoe looks lost.
- Oversized top + short skirt + flat bare shoe → top-heavy (formula E needs weight at the leg).
- Every piece fuzzy or soft → looks like sleepwear.
- Two competing volumes at the same level (puffy sleeve + bulky vest).
- Low-rise bottom with a cropped top in formula C → crop line lost.
- Low-rise baggy bottoms + a long loose top + no hip anchor → shapeless, no center.
- Baggy hem too long and thin-soled shoe, so it drags under the heel. Fix: cuff or
  use a heavier shoe.
- Baggy jeans + delicate shoe (ballet flat, thin sandal).
- Two graphics or logos (graphic tee + embroidered jeans + logo beanie).
- Plaid + stripe + graphic in the same outfit.
- Tie with a graphic or text tee on top, with no collar visible → the tie looks
  random.
- Mini skirt + oversized top + flat shoe (top-heavy; needs boots).
- Fur or puffer + baggy bottoms + large bag → three volumes.
- Off-shoulder oversized top + oversized bottoms → no waist or shoulder line.
- Low-rise flare + loose, long top → the flare is lost.
- Long coat that hides a mini skirt or shorts entirely (looks like no bottoms) when
  the user wants the skirt visible. Fix: shorter jacket.
- Mixing silver and gold hardware in the dark palette (belt studs silver, jewelry
  gold) unless the user asks for it.

## Step 5: Adjust to the person and the occasion

Treat these as goals, not restrictions. Follow the user's stated preferences first.

- **Petite:** keep crop and rise rules strict; limit pooling; platforms help;
  formula F lengthens. In G, cuff the jean and keep the top cropped; in I and J use
  the heel (boot or platform) to lengthen; avoid very long baggy hems that stack.
- **Tall:** can carry more pooling, longer layers and midi/maxi lengths, long
  shorts and over-the-knee boots.
- **Wants to show the waist:** prefer D, A, or C with a belt.
- **Prefers a relaxed fit at the middle:** prefer F or B with a long open layer.
- **Office / formal:** keep the formula, swap the pieces: tailored wide trousers
  instead of printed jeans; a loafer or block heel instead of chunky sneakers;
  drop playful extras (ties, leg warmers) unless the user wants them.
- **School / casual daily wear:** H with crew socks, sneakers, mini crossbody.
- **Evening / going out:** I with heels or tall boots, a drape top or fitted knit,
  and a small shoulder bag.
- **Cold weather:** outer layer must follow the same rules. A cropped boxy jacket
  works with wide legs (C); a long coat calls for F or a slim leg (B). Fur-trim or
  puffer cropped jacket over G or J; shearling mini boots with baggy jeans; tights
  with I.
- **Warm weather:** tank or crop top with baggy jeans or cargo shorts (G); mini skirt
  with crew socks and sneakers.

---

## Output format

For each outfit:
1. Items, with key tags (e.g. "oversized bouclé sweater, cropped at waist").
2. Variant and formula name (A–J).
3. One sentence on why it works (the waist or hip anchor, how the hem meets the shoe).
4. Optional: one swap if the user wants it dressier or more casual.

## Worked examples

**1 · Formula C**
Red bouclé off-shoulder sweater, oversized, cropped at waist, balloon sleeves;
fitted black sleeveless turtleneck underneath. High-rise wide-leg jeans (painted
print). Long red sash belt. Chunky white sneakers.
Why: crop plus sash mark the waist inside the volume; the turtleneck shows at the
shoulder and waist; heavy sneakers anchor the wide leg.

**2 · Formula A**
Fitted sheer white long-sleeve + black wrap cami tied at the waist with long ties.
High-rise baggy splatter-print jeans. Chunky sneakers.
Why: the slim top lets the printed leg lead; wrap ties mark the waist and add a
vertical line.

**3 · Formula A (soft variant)**
Relaxed chartreuse raglan sweater cropped to high hip, gathered cuffs. High-rise
flares, fitted through the thigh, covering the shoe. Studded wide belt slung at
the hip. Platform shearling mini boots.
Why: the short sweater shows the fitted thigh, so the flare's shape is visible;
the platform carries the hem.

**4 · Formula C (layered)**
Boxy cropped sherpa jacket over an oxford shirt tied at the front, white tee,
striped tie, brown belt. Dark wide straight jeans, pooling. Lace-up lug boots.
Why: the tied shirt and belt make a waist point under the boxy jacket; the tie
adds a vertical line; lug boots match the weight.

**5 · Formula D**
Burgundy polo-collar knit cropped at the natural waist, balloon sleeves, white
collar and black tie. High-waist pleated black midi skirt. Chunky sneakers with socks.
Why: the knit ends exactly at the skirt waistband, so the waist is the narrowest point.

**6 · Formula E**
Charcoal oversized cropped sweater, white shirt collar and hem showing, red
leather skinny tie. Black A-line mini skort. Tall black biker boots.
Why: the bare leg is the slim section; knee boots bring weight back so the
oversized top doesn't dominate.

**7 · Formula H**
Oversized graphic sweatshirt over a white collared shirt, long tie hanging between the
layers. Black baggy jeans, pooling. Black sneakers. Mini black bag.
Why: the shirt hem and tie show under the sweatshirt; the graphic is the only
pattern; the baggy leg falls over a heavy sneaker.

**8 · Formula H (tied plaid)**
White cropped button shirt over a white tank. Blue plaid shirt tied at the hip. Light
baggy jeans. Chunky sneakers.
Why: the cropped shirt ends at the plaid anchor; plaid is the single pattern; the jean
covers the heavy sneaker.

**9 · Formula H (jersey)**
Red and navy striped rugby sweatshirt over a shirt, tie visible at the collar. Baggy
cargo shorts. White crew socks. Skate sneakers. Red mini bag.
Why: the collar and tie break the stripe at the neck; the bag repeats the red; shorts
need the sock-and-sneaker weight.

**10 · Formula G**
White crop tank. Low-rise black wide graphic jeans with a belt. Chunky black sneakers.
Red mini crossbody.
Why: the belt and the waistband are the anchor; the graphic is on the jeans only; the
red bag is the accent.

**11 · Formula G (warm neutrals)**
Black oversized off-shoulder sweater with ruched sleeves. Raw dark wide jeans, cuffed.
Brown retro-runner sneakers. Beanie. Gold layered necklaces and a gold watch. Brown
ruched bag.
Why: the neckline shows the shoulder, so the sweater has a line; the cuff makes
a clean hem above the sneaker; brown and gold repeat across shoe, bag and jewelry.

**12 · Formula G (alt)**
Graphic long-sleeve tee under a black tank. Black skull-print wide jeans. Studded belt.
Black skate sneakers. Black shoulder bag.
Why: black palette with texture and print on the jeans only; the studded belt is the
anchor.

**13 · Formula I**
Brown suede halter top, flared at the hem. Brown mini skirt. Slouchy olive-brown boots.
Tan fringe bag.
Why: the halter ends above the skirt; the slouchy boot gives the leg weight; one
brown family.

**14 · Formula I (drape)**
Grey off-shoulder drape top. Denim shorts. Brown knee boots, tan hobo bag.
Why: the drape top is the volume; the shorts show the leg; the boots have a visible
shaft and carry the weight.

**15 · Formula I (coat)**
Camel wrap trench, open. Black shorts. Black over-the-knee boots. Cap.
Why: the long coat is the vertical line; the black shorts and boots are one column
under it; the cap is the finishing detail.

**16 · Formula J**
Black cropped zip jacket. White tank. Low-rise dark flare jeans. Heeled boots.
Why: the jacket ends at the waist; the tank and waistband show; the flare covers
most of the boot.

**17 · Formula J (fur)**
Faux fur short jacket. White tank. White low-rise flares. Heeled shoes. Sunglasses.
Why: the fur is the only volume; the fitted tank and the flare make a clean lower
half; tonal white keeps it light.

**18 · Formula G (winter)**
Black cropped hooded puffer over a grey zip hoodie. Washed baggy jeans. Shearling
mini boots. Small black bag.
Why: the cropped puffer ends at the waist; the baggy jean stacks over the boot; the
bag is small.
