# Silhouette matching: Casual style

A casual outfit looks effortless only when it is balanced. Usually one piece (the
coat, blazer or sweatshirt) carries the volume, and something else gives the outfit
an anchor: a shirt hem, a tuck, a heavy shoe, a collar. Without that, relaxed becomes
shapeless. Never pick items by name pairs ("wide trousers go with X"). Tag each item,
choose a formula, apply the rules, then check for failure modes.

## Scope

This skill describes **casual everyday style**: relaxed city and campus dressing.
Typical pieces are oversized long coats and blazers, wide or parachute trousers, baggy
and straight jeans, oversized sweatshirts, hoodies and knits, Breton stripes, long
skirts (lace, plaid, parachute), pleated minis with knee socks, leather and suede
jackets, caps, beanies and berets, sneakers and chunky loafers, and a medium tote.

- If the user wants this style (or words like casual, everyday, relaxed, comfy,
  effortless, city, campus, errands), use this skill as written.
- If the user wants a different style (classy/tailored, minimalism, street, evening),
  keep the proportion principles (one hero volume, anchor point, hem and shoe), but
  don't add this style's signature pieces (sweatshirt over a maxi skirt, track pants
  with a blazer, knee socks with loafers).

## Step 2: Choose one formula and name it

**A. Long layer over relaxed base**
Long coat, oversized blazer or leather jacket over a tee or knit and straight or
baggy jeans (or wide trousers).
Requires: the coat is the only oversized piece; the base underneath is regular or slim;
trouser hem falls over the shoe; a visible edge (tee hem, collar, cuff).
Why: the long vertical line of the coat lengthens the figure, and the relaxed base keeps
it casual.

**B. Wide leg, fitted or tucked top**
Wide, parachute or pleated trousers with a fitted tee or knit, or a shirt under a
V-neck, tucked in.
Requires: mid or high rise; the top is tucked or ends at the waist or high hip;
the hem covers most of the shoe (or a deliberate crop with a loafer).
Why: the fitted top shows the waist, and the wide leg is the statement.

**C. Soft volume on wide leg, anchored**
Oversized sweatshirt, tee or striped top with wide or baggy trousers.
Requires ALL of:
1. Top ends at the high hip or waist, or is half-tucked, and does not cover the rise.
2. A visible anchor: shirt or tee hem, belt, tuck, or a contrasting layer.
3. Mid or high rise.
4. Chunky or substantial shoe (retro runner, chunky sneaker, lug loafer).
Why: the hem line and the heavy shoe give the body a shape inside the volume.

**D. Long skirt, oversized top**
Oversized sweatshirt, knit or blazer over a maxi or midi skirt (lace, parachute, plaid,
knit).
Requires: the top ends at the waist or high hip; a shirt or tee hem peeks out below it, or
the skirt is high-waisted; chunky sneakers, boots or lug loafers.
Why: the skirt's long line balances the top, and the heavy shoe keeps it casual.

**E. Tonal knit column**
Knit top with a knit or wool midi skirt or wide trousers in the same color family
(grey on grey, black on black), with a coat, blazer or leather jacket over it.
Requires: two or more fabrics or tones in the column (knit, wool, leather,
pinstripe); chunky loafer or boot; sock or boot edge visible.
Why: one tone lengthens, and the texture keeps it from looking flat.

**F. Preppy mini and socks**
Pleated or plaid mini skirt with a sweater or blazer, shirt collar (and tie), knee
socks, and loafers or Mary Janes.
Requires: the top ends at or above the skirt waistband (or is a cropped
blazer); collar visible at the neck; knee socks or boots between hem and shoe; heavy
or medium shoe.
Why: the skirt is short and the socks and shoe bring the weight back, so the outfit
isn't top-heavy.

**G. Sporty with tailored**
Track pants (with side stripe), hoodie or sweatshirt, paired with one tailored piece:
an oversized blazer, wool coat or loafer.
Requires: exactly one tailored piece; a visible shirt or tee hem; sneaker or loafer.
Why: the tailoring lifts the sporty pieces, so it reads as styled, not as loungewear.

## Step 3: Apply the core rules

1. **One hero volume.** One oversized piece (coat, blazer, sweatshirt or trousers) carries
   the volume. A second large piece needs an anchor (rule 2).
2. **Anchor point.** A tuck, a visible shirt or tee hem, a belt, a tied shirt at the
   hip, or a collar. An oversized outfit with no anchor is a failure.
3. **Hem and shoe.** Straight, baggy and wide hems fall over the shoe and may pool
   slightly. Cropped wide trousers need a deliberate gap with a loafer or sock.
   Never a hem that stops halfway down the shoe.
4. **Shoe weight follows volume.** Big volume needs a retro runner, chunky sneaker,
   lug loafer or boot. Delicate shoes only with slim, fitted outfits.
5. **Rise.** Mid or high rise with wide, parachute and pleated trousers; mid rise with
   baggy jeans.
6. **Layers show at the edges.** Collar at the neck, shirt hem below a sweater, tee hem
   below a hoodie, cuff at the wrist. Adds lines without bulk.
7. **Palette: neutral base plus one accent.** Neutrals (grey, charcoal, black, navy,
   brown, cream, khaki, olive). One accent (burgundy, red, light blue, green) in one
   place, or two that echo (maroon beret and maroon bag).
8. **One pattern.** Breton stripe, pinstripe, plaid or leopard, not two. Leopard works
   as a neutral: pair it with solid neutrals and one plain top.
9. **Texture contrast.** Soft knit or sweat with structured wool, denim, leather or
   suede. An outfit that is all fleece and jersey looks like loungewear.
10. **Long coat rule.** The coat ends at mid-calf or longer; the trouser hem goes below
    it or the skirt is shorter than the coat is long. A coat ending at the knee over
    a midi skirt creates a cluttered line.
11. **Skirt rule.** Maxi and midi skirts go with chunky sneakers, boots or loafers;
    minis go with knee socks or boots. Delicate heels are out.
12. **Headwear is the finishing piece.** Cap, beanie or beret, one only, in the accent
    or a neutral. Sunglasses are fine with any of them.
13. **Bag.** Medium tote or soft shoulder bag in brown, black or burgundy; it echoes
    the shoe or the accent. One bag only.
14. **Sporty pieces need one tailored piece** (rule from formula G).

## Step 4: Check failure modes (reject or fix)

- Oversized coat + oversized sweater + baggy trousers, no hem or tuck anchor → shapeless.
- Hoodie or sweatshirt + sweatpants + sneakers with no tailored or structured piece →
  looks like loungewear.
- Hem stops halfway down the shoe, or is too short for a wide leg.
- Big volume with a delicate shoe (thin flat, strappy sandal).
- Two patterns (stripe + leopard, plaid + pinstripe).
- Two or more accent colors competing.
- Long coat + maxi skirt + large bag → three long or heavy shapes.
- Knit midi skirt + knit sweater + knit coat, all soft and the same texture → flat.
- Hood + scarf + big collar at the neck → bulk at the same level.
- Mini skirt + oversized blazer + flat bare shoe (needs socks or boots).
- Cropped wide trousers with sneakers that show a bare ankle gap by accident
  (needs deliberate socks or loafers).
- Too many accessories: beanie + scarf + sunglasses + large bag + stacked jewelry.

---

## Step 5: Adjust to the person and the occasion

Treat these as goals, not restrictions. Follow the user's stated preferences first.

- **Petite:** use a hip-length oversized blazer or short coat (A) instead of a very long
  one; keep trousers high rise and pooling minimal; tonal column (E) lengthens; avoid
  maxi skirts with flat shoes.
- **Tall:** can carry floor-length coats, maxi skirts, pooling hems and very wide trousers.
- **Wants to show the waist:** prefer B, with a tucked tee or a belt over a shirt.
- **Prefers a relaxed middle:** prefer A, D or G with an untucked shirt hem.
- **Office-casual:** keep the formula; blazer or wool coat, straight or wide trousers,
  chunky loafer, a clean knit under; no hood, no graphic.
- **Campus / errands:** C, D or F with a retro runner or high-top, a medium tote,
  a cap or beanie.
- **Cold weather:** coat or puffer vest over the formula; boots or lug loafers with
  socks; beanie; knit skirt with a coat (E).
- **Warm weather:** drop the coat; a tee or Breton top with wide trousers or a maxi
  skirt, low-profile sneakers, cap.

## Output format

For each outfit:
1. Items, with key tags (e.g. "oversized grey wool coat, long; white tee; straight jeans, pooling").
2. Formula name (A–G).
3. One sentence on why it works (the hero volume, the anchor, the hem and shoe).
4. Optional: one swap if the user wants it dressier or sportier.

## Worked examples

**1 · Formula A**
Oversized grey wool long coat. White tee. Straight jeans, pooling over the shoe. Red
sneakers. Red cap.
Why: the coat is the single volume; the tee shows at the neckline and gives a light
edge; the red echoes at the head and the foot.

**2 · Formula C**
Burgundy beret. Oversized striped long-sleeve top, ending at the high hip. Olive
parachute wide trousers, high rise. Retro sneakers. Brown tote.
Why: the top ends at the hip, so the waist is not lost; stripe is the only pattern; the
sneakers carry the wide leg; the beret is the accent.

**3 · Formula G**
Cap. Oversized grey blazer over a grey knit vest and a light blue shirt (cuff and hem
showing). Black track pants with a white side stripe. Sneakers.
Why: the blazer is the one tailored piece, so the track pants read as styled; the shirt
cuff and hem are the edge layers.

**4 · Formula D**
Oversized grey-green sweatshirt over a white long-sleeve (cuff and hem showing). White
lace maxi skirt. Chunky sneakers.
Why: the sweatshirt ends at the high hip; the white hem and cuff add line; the sneakers
keep the lace casual.

**5 · Formula D (blazer)**
Oversized grey blazer, black tee. Grey plaid maxi skirt. Black knee boots. Medium
shoulder bag.
Why: the blazer is the hero; the plaid skirt is the only pattern; the boot gives the
long skirt weight at the hem.

**6 · Formula D (parachute)**
Black oversized sweatshirt over a white shirt, hem showing. Black parachute maxi skirt.
Chunky black-and-white sneakers.
Why: the white shirt hem is the anchor against an all-black outfit; the sneakers add
weight.

**7 · Formula E**
Grey knit sweater. Grey knit midi skirt. Black long coat, open. White crew socks.
Chunky black loafers. Black structured bag.
Why: a tonal knit column under a long coat; the white sock and chunky loafer mark the
hem; the coat gives the vertical line.

**8 · Formula E (suit set)**
Grey pinstripe oversized blazer with matching wide trousers. Grey tee. Chunky black
shoes.
Why: a tonal set; the pinstripe is the only pattern; the tee is plain; the heavy shoe
matches the wide trouser.

**9 · Formula B**
Black V-neck sweater over a white shirt, collar and cuffs showing. Olive pleated wide
trousers, cropped. Black loafers.
Why: the fitted knit ends at the waist; the crop leaves a deliberate gap with loafers;
the shirt collar is the edge layer.

**10 · Formula B (leopard)**
Light blue shirt under a navy blazer. Leopard wide trousers, high rise. White
sneakers.
Why: leopard is the single pattern and acts as a neutral; the blazer and shirt are
solid; the sneakers are plain.

**11 · Formula A (leather)**
Oversized distressed leather jacket. Grey sweater. Grey wide trousers. Burgundy loafers.
Why: the jacket is the volume and the texture contrast; the sweater and trousers are
a tonal base; the burgundy loafer is the accent.

**12 · Formula A (long coat)**
Long brown wool coat. Blue shirt, collar showing. Light-wash straight jeans, pooling.
Burgundy retro sneakers. Leopard tote.
Why: the long coat is the hero; the light blue and light wash lighten it; leopard is
only on the bag.

**13 · Formula C (denim)**
Striped beanie. Oversized denim jacket in a mid wash. Wide light-wash jeans. Patterned
clutch.
Why: two different washes keep denim on denim from looking like a set; the jacket ends
at the high hip; the clutch is the single pattern.

**14 · Formula F**
Grey pleated mini skirt. Tweed blazer, cropped. Shirt and tie, collar showing. Knee
socks. Chunky loafers.
Why: the blazer ends at the skirt waistband; the tie adds a vertical line; the knee
socks and loafers bring weight back at the leg.
