# Style Knowledge v0.1

Short rules for the Virtual Stylist Agent. They are defaults, not laws. The user's own rules win.
Read this before building outfits.

## 1. Start from the task

Ask for the occasion, the dress code, the weather, how long the person will walk or sit, and any comfort or
modesty rules. If something is unknown, ask. Never guess the weather or the dress code.

## 2. Colour

- Start with one neutral base: black, navy, grey, beige, white or brown.
- Add 1-2 supporting colours and at most one accent. Three colours in one outfit is a simple limit, not a law.
- Monochrome (one colour in different shades) looks intentional and is easy to wear.
- A neutral outfit with one accent (bag, shoes, scarf) is the safest way to look put together.
- Use one print at a time; keep the other pieces solid.
- Dark and light together create contrast. Use it to separate the top and the bottom.

## 3. Proportion and line

- Balance volume: a loose top with a straighter bottom, or a fitted top with wide trousers. All-volume can work
  when it is deliberate.
- Show the waist when it helps: tuck the top, add a belt, choose a high rise or a cropped jacket.
- Use the rule of thirds: about one third on top and two thirds on the bottom, or the reverse.
- Keep one focal point per outfit. If four things compete, remove one.
- A column of one colour (top and bottom) makes the line look longer.

## 4. Texture and layers

- Mix 2-3 textures (knit, denim, leather, cotton, silk), but keep one dominant.
- Layer in three: base + middle + outer.
- Cold: base layer, knit or fleece, coat, warm shoes, scarf. Cover the ankles if it is windy.
- Warm: one light breathable layer, a looser fit, light colours and shade. Carry a thin layer for the evening.
- Shoes set the formality: sneakers = casual, loafers or flats or ankle boots = smart casual, heels or closed dress
  shoes = formal.

## 5. Dress codes

| Code | Safe formula | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Casual | jeans or trousers + t-shirt or hoodie + sneakers | dirty or damaged clothes |
| Smart casual | blazer or cardigan + shirt or knit + dark jeans or trousers + loafers, flats or ankle boots | hoodies, graphic tees, flip-flops for a strict event |
| Business | suit or tailored separates + closed shoes + minimal accessories | very casual fabrics, loud prints |
| Formal | suit or dress + closed dress shoes + small bag | denim, sneakers, heavy casual layers |
| University presentation | blazer or clean jacket + shirt or knit + trousers or dark jeans + closed shoes | beachwear, very short hems, loud patterns |
| Date | one focal point + comfortable shoes + a layer for the evening | clothes that need constant adjustment |

Smart casual is a mix of polished and relaxed pieces, not a fixed uniform. Check the place and the people; if unsure,
ask the organiser.

## 6. Outfit formulas

- Jeans + t-shirt + blazer + sneakers or loafers.
- Trousers + knit + long coat + ankle boots.
- Midi skirt + blouse + cardigan + flats.
- Dress + jacket + boots.
- Hoodie + jeans + coat + sneakers (casual only).
- One-colour column + accent bag or shoes.

## 7. Wardrobe balance

- A capsule is not a fixed 20-item list. For autumn/winter 2026-27, start with 2 outer layers
  (a long coat or trench + a puffer or windbreaker), 2-3 knits, 3 tops, 2-3 bottoms, 2 shoes and
  2-3 accessories; add one statement piece.
- A useful mix is about 70% everyday essentials and 30% personality pieces. One statement piece is
  enough; do not force every trend into one outfit.
- Three tops, three bottoms and three pairs of shoes already give 27 combinations.
- The current trend base is `KNOWLEDGE/trends-2026-27.json`; check its `use_until` date before using it.

## 8. Students in Kazakhstan

- Check the forecast for the city and the day. Mornings and evenings can differ, so layers help.
- Long campus walks need comfortable shoes, a bag that fits A4 and a layer you can take off indoors.
- For family or official events, ask about modesty and coverage. Do not assume.
- Prices in tenge (₸) only if the user asks about shopping. v0.1 styles owned clothes only.

## 9. Body-neutral language

- Talk about the garment: fit, colour, line, texture, occasion and comfort.
- Never say "slimming", "hides your figure" or "flattering for your shape".
- If a garment does not fit, say "this size or cut may pull at the shoulder" and suggest another item, not a body
  change.
- Body shape is optional. Use it only when the user gives measurements or names a shape; follow
  `SKILLS/bodyshape/SKILL.md`. Never guess it from a photo or a size.
- Shape guidance is about the garment's line: "a straight trouser gives a clean vertical line", not
  "this makes you look thinner". The user's comfort and taste win.

## 10. Checklist before answering

- Only items the user owns.
- Three outfits: Safe, Sharper and Comfortable.
- Each outfit has a reason and one watch-out.
- The weather is answered.
- No shopping links and no body talk.
- End with one adjustment question.
